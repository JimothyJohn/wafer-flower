LOOK

What the design actually controls

The piece is nine identical stations: a Ø300 silicon wafer bonded to a hidden printed segment, arranged in an overlapping swirl. Three things set how it reads on the wall:

  1. Tilt θ — how far the swirl stands off the wall and how three-dimensional it reads. This is the big one, and it's settled physics that it's aesthetic: even θ=5° gives a 14.3 mm neighbor gap against a 3 mm requirement, and droop is ~0.01 mm either way. θ buys depth — 26.9 mm at 5°, 52.9 at 10°, 78.4 at 15° — at ~4 % assembly mass and ~5 % dovetail margin per degree.
  2. The hide window — the frame must be invisible from the front. Every wafer's footprint projects as an ellipse; at the joint meridian the neighbors' ellipses overlap over a radial range (≈ 240–416 mm at θ=10°), and the band plus the gear tips must stay inside it. The calculator flags violations live; the closed form is on the OnShape variables page.
  3. Wafer size and count — Ø300 at N=9 is the standard build (995 mm halo OD at θ=10°). Smaller wafers scale the whole piece down; N sets how tight the swirl winds and how much each wafer overlaps its neighbor.
Where Rev B.3 landed
Current build: θ=5°, N=9, R=350, band 255–285, t_min=10. θ=5 wasn't picked purely on looks — it's forced by the user-set 30 mm part-thickness cap at N=9 (θ=7.5° needs 32.1 mm, θ=10° needs 39.6). If the deeper θ=10 look is wanted back, N=12 allows it at 27.9 mm — the swirl winds tighter and the segments shrink. The cap also fixed the build's worst structural margin: dovetail 2.0× → 4.5×.
θ

Settling θ = 5 vs 10

This was the open question, and the statics answer it cleanly: θ is an aesthetic choice, and every structural metric prefers the smaller one. Ø300, N=9, band centered under the wafer CG:

θEnvelope depthNeighbor z-gapDroop (hung)Assembly massDovetail margin
5°26.9 mm14.3 mm0.01 mm2.31 kg2.0×
7.5°39.9 mm21.5 mm0.01 mm2.60 kg1.8×
10°52.9 mm28.9 mm0.02 mm2.90 kg1.6×
15°78.4 mm44.2 mm0.02 mm3.48 kg1.3×

The z-gap argument for a larger θ evaporates: even θ=5 gives 14.3 mm of neighbor gap against a 3 mm requirement, and droop is ~0.01 mm either way. What θ actually buys is depth — how far the piece stands off the wall and how much the swirl reads as three-dimensional — at a cost of ~4 % assembly mass and ~5 % joint margin per degree (slicer-calibrated masses — the solid shells dilute the old uniform-density trade). Land arc is 36° of 40° at every θ in this range, so bond area isn't in the trade either.

Recommendation from the numbers: keep Ø300 — mass is a non-issue (2–3 kg) and it's the look you want. Pick θ purely on how deep you want the piece to sit; θ=10 is a fine choice and costs ~26 % more mass than θ=5, but nothing structural forces it. Move the band outboard toward the wafer CG only in the same revision that fixes the joint. And fix the dovetail first — it is the only number in this document under 40×, and every other improvement makes it worse.

The one rule design cannot bend
Gear tooth count must divide by N or the ring-gear pitch breaks at every joint, and at module 2 / N=9 the clean 252-tooth fit pins Ri ≈ 255. Band moves are negotiated with that constraint — the calculator's Gear tips vs hide window readout referees.
