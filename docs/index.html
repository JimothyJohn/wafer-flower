01 — THE ILLUSION

The frame is designed to disappear.

Each wafer tilts 5° about its own radial axis, leading edge proud, so nine discs shingle into one continuous swirl. The printed ring that carries them sits entirely inside the hide window — the band of wall that every front sightline misses behind the overlapping silicon. What the room reads is a floating, turning iris.

02 — THE DRIVE

A 108-tooth gear, hiding behind the silicon.

A 45° bevel ring gear is generated straight into the segments — 12 teeth per segment, 108 around, the pattern tiling seamlessly across every joint. A 20-tooth pinion engages from the top at 5.4 : 1, and the whole ring hangs on two flanged bearings riding an internal groove directly under the motor. Torque demand is a few millinewton-metres: one small gearmotor, PWM-dialed to a gallery-slow two revolutions per minute.

03 — THE BOND

Taped, not glued — the numbers say tape wins.

Each wafer lands once on acrylic foam tape. Gravity loads the bond at 0.13 kPa against ~500 kPa of capability; the load that actually governs is thermal — silicon and plastic breathing at different rates — and even that sits ~47× under the limit across a 20 K swing. The 1.1 mm foam bondline absorbs the mismatch a rigid epoxy would drive straight into the wafer.

04 — THE BUILD

Nine identical prints, dovetailed in one direction.

One segment design, printed nine times — 69 g and 2h20 each — sliding together on dovetails in Z, no tools, no fasteners. A printed bench jig funnels each wafer onto its tape in a single guided drop, centred by four chamfered pegs to a ±0.15 mm window. Every solid is CSG-generated and gate-checked in CI before it earns a print.
